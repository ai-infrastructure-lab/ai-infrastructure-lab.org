Our work combines three strands: AI industry research, technology assessment, and infrastructure studies. By tracking developments in this industry sector and evaluating the technical, societal and ecological footprint of new systems, we aim to understand the real-world impact of AI technologies on the way we live and work together. We build on these insights to contribute to debates on European digital sovereignty, rethinking how technological advancement can align with sustainable, localized control over critical infrastructure and sovereign data.

It is hosted at the [Centre of Language and Speech Technology, Radboud University, The Netherlands](https://www.ru.nl/en/cls/clst).

# What we do
Often using fieldwork-based and empirical methods, the lab investigates software and hardware ecosystems that power artificial intelligence, assessing their societal and economic implications. 

## AI Industry Research
Tracking developments in the AI industry sector to map evolving market dynamics, identifying business (mal)practices, and strategic shifts within the global ecosystem. 

## Technology Assessment 
Evaluate the technical, societal, and ecological footprints of AI models as well as related infrastructures to separate industry hype from practical reality. Past work in this strand includes: Openness and model transparency assessment in the field of 'open source AI', including related regulatory and standardisation processes; Assessment of privacy concerns in voice AI technology. 

## Infrastructure Studies
Developing frameworks to understand large scale AI-related infrastructure such as fibre-optic networks and datacenters, and ,often rooted in antitrust economics, rethinking how technological advancement can align with sustainable, localized control over critical infrastructure and sovereign data to empower organizations and policymakers.
